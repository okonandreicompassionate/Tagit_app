The bit everyone already finds annoying

You're at a concert. You meet someone good. Out come the phones — you spell your username, they type it wrong, you try again, someone's torch is in your face, the song drops, and you both give up and say "I'll find you."

You don't find them. And the ones you do add turn into a row of strangers in your followers list, because a username on its own tells you nothing about who they were or why you liked them.

How a night actually goes

Three things happen, in this order. Each one only works because of the one before it.

1

You walk in and tap or scan the code on the door

Every venue or host puts up a code. You scan it and that's you checked in — Tagit now knows you were genuinely at that event, on that night.

This is the part nobody can fake. You can't check in from your bedroom.

2

You meet someone, and you scan them

The app opens straight to the camera. Point it at their code and their card appears — name, photo, and one big button: Add on Snap. One tap opens Snapchat with them already there. Nobody spells anything.

3

Next week, you still know who they were

Everyone you scan is saved with the night attached: met at Flytime Fest, Saturday, the one who does photography. Instead of a nameless follower, you get the memory back.

Run into them again at another event and it starts a streak — a record of a friendship that keeps happening in real life.

Coming next

Stop scanning. Just tap.

Cameras are the slow part. In a dark, packed room you're squinting, holding a phone steady, waiting for focus. Tapping takes no aim and no light — it works with one hand holding a drink.

NFC

Tap a wristband to check in

The venue puts a sticker on the door, or builds it into the wristband. Tap it, you're checked in. No camera, no queue, no fumbling in the dark.

Tap a Tagit card to swap details

A card the size of a bank card, a keyring, or a sticker on the back of your phone. Someone taps their phone on it and your profile opens — even if they've never heard of Tagit.

Tap a poster to find the event

Flyers on a wall with a tap point. Brands get a physical way into the app, and you get the event without typing anything.

How it actually works

An NFC sticker is a tiny chip with no battery, powered by the phone held near it. All it stores is a short web link — the exact same link that's inside a Tagit QR code today.

So the phone doesn't need to understand Tagit. It reads a link and opens it. Your phone supplies the power, the sticker supplies the address.

Why it's cheap to do

The links already exist and already work. Nothing about check-ins, points or profiles has to change — NFC is a second doorway into the same system.

Blank stickers cost pennies in bulk. A host writes their event's link onto a pack of them from inside the app.

One thing it can't do — worth knowing now

Two phones can't tap each other. Everyone assumes they can, but iPhones can't pretend to be an NFC tag at all, and Android removed phone-to-phone tapping years ago. Any app promising it is either using something else under the hood, or not working.

So NFC is for things — wristbands, cards, stickers, posters. Phone-to-phone stays the camera, which already works everywhere and needs nothing extra. Tap where there's something to tap; scan otherwise.
